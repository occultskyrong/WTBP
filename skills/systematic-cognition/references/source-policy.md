# Source and Evidence Rules

## Source Levels

| Level | Sources | Use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Official documentation, laws and regulations, standards, papers, primary data, announcements, and original-author material | Definitions, rules, product capabilities, and formal facts |
| B | Universities, research institutions, professional media, and industry reports | Supplementary explanation and independent verification |
| C | High-quality blogs, interviews, and technical communities | Experience, examples, or leads; phrase cautiously |
| D | Forums, short videos, anonymous articles, and aggregated summaries | Search leads only; never sole support for a key conclusion |

## Evidence Records

For every important conclusion, record at least:

- A reviewable original URL.
- Page title plus publication or update date; use access date when no date is available.
- The concrete page content or location supporting the conclusion; do not infer from a title.
- Source level and applicable scope.
- Whether an independent source cross-check exists.

## Quality Thresholds

- Every key fact needs at least one direct primary source; high-risk or disputed facts should have two independent sources where possible.
- Search snippets, reposts, aggregation pages, and model memory do not replace a primary source.
- If a source supports only part of a conclusion, retain only the supported part.
- When sources conflict, retain both, label time and definitions, and state what cannot be confirmed.
- Broken links, inaccessible pages, or unverified citations are not confirmed evidence.
